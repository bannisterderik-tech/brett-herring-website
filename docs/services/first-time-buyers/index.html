First-Time Buyers

You probably qualify for more help than you think.

Oregon has real programs for first-time buyers. Below market rate loans through the state bond program, down payment assistance at the state and local level, and homebuyer education classes that unlock the rest. Most renters never find out because nobody ever walked them through it.

Start with the number, not the house

The first conversation is with a lender, and it is free. You will learn what you qualify for, which programs you fit, and what your actual monthly payment looks like with taxes and insurance included. Some people learn they can buy today. Some learn they need to clean up one account and wait four months. Both answers beat guessing.

Down payment help is real

Oregon Housing and Community Services runs Flex Lending, which comes with down payment assistance attached, plus a separate larger assistance program for first-time and first-generation buyers that is administered locally. In Lane County that local administrator is DevNW, with an office in Springfield. Amounts, income limits, and funding availability change, which is exactly why you ask rather than assume. The rules are knowable. Whether you qualify is knowable. It takes one phone call.

What the classes actually do

Homebuyer education gets treated like a hoop to jump through. It is not. It is a few hours that will teach you how escrow works, what closing costs really are, and how to not get taken. It also unlocks assistance programs that require it. Locally there are options both online and in person.

Buying is not always the right answer

If you are moving in eighteen months, if your job is unstable, or if buying would wipe out your entire savings with nothing left for a water heater, renting another year is the smarter play. I will tell you that, and I will still be here when the timing works. An agent who only ever says "buy now" is not advising you.

FAQ

First-Time Buyers: straight answers

Do I really need twenty percent down?

No. This is the most common and most expensive misconception in the whole process. Many loan programs allow far less, and Oregon down payment assistance can help with what remains. Ask a lender what you actually qualify for before you write off buying.

What credit score do I need to buy a house?

There are loan paths at a range of credit levels, and the cutoff people imagine is usually higher than reality. If your credit needs work, a good lender will tell you exactly which two or three things to fix and roughly how long it takes. That is a plan, not a rejection.

What are closing costs and who pays them?

Closing costs cover lender fees, title, escrow, prepaid taxes and insurance, and recording. They typically run a few percent of the purchase price. Some can be negotiated into the deal, some can be covered by assistance programs, and all of them should be shown to you in writing well before closing.

Are first-time buyer programs only for people who have never owned?

Often "first-time buyer" means you have not owned a primary residence in the last three years, which surprises a lot of people who owned once years ago. Definitions vary by program. Worth checking rather than assuming you are disqualified.
