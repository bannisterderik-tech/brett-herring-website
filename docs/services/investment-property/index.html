Investment

Run the numbers before you fall for the property.

A rental is a business with a roof. It either pencils or it does not, and the way to find out is arithmetic done before you are emotionally invested. Purchase price, realistic rent, vacancy, taxes, insurance, management, maintenance reserve, and what happens the year the roof goes.

The numbers that decide it

Gross rent is the least interesting number in the analysis. What matters is what is left after taxes, insurance, vacancy, maintenance reserve, management, and debt service. A property that cash flows on paper and not in reality is usually a property where somebody skipped the reserve line.

Condition matters more on a rental

You will not be there to notice the slow leak. Big systems near the end of their life are the difference between a steady asset and a series of emergencies. On a rental, spend the inspection money and read the report twice.

Know the rules before you buy

Oregon has statewide landlord tenant law that is more tenant protective than many states, including notice requirements and limits on rent increases. None of that makes rentals a bad idea. All of it makes going in informed a very good idea.

Management is a real cost or a real job

Either you pay someone a percentage or you take the calls yourself. Both are legitimate. What does not work is budgeting for neither and then discovering the hard way that a rental generates work.

FAQ

Investment: straight answers

Does a rental in Lane County actually cash flow?

Some do, some do not, and the difference is the purchase price and the condition. The analysis is straightforward and I will run it honestly, including on deals I would not do myself.

What expenses do first-time investors forget?

Vacancy, capital reserves for roof and HVAC, and management. Skipping those three makes almost any property look profitable on a spreadsheet and disappointing in real life.

Are there landlord rules I should know about in Oregon?

Yes. Oregon has statewide rules covering notice, rent increase limits, and tenant protections. Talk to a property manager or attorney before your first purchase so you start compliant.

Is a duplex a good first investment?

Small multifamily can be a good entry point, particularly if you live in one unit. It is not automatically better than a single family rental. The specific numbers decide it.
