Your house gets one first week. Spend it well.

Every listing gets a burst of attention when it hits the market. What happens in that window sets the tone for everything after. Prepared, priced right, photographed properly, and it moves. Rushed onto the market to see what happens, and you spend the next two months chasing it down in price.

Price is a strategy, not a wish

The listing price is not what you want, and it is not what an algorithm guessed. It is a decision about who walks through the door in the first ten days. Price it correctly and you create competition. Price it high to leave negotiating room and you mostly buy yourself a price reduction and a stale listing, which is the weakest negotiating position there is.

What actually moves the number

Not the granite. The stuff buyers get scared by: a roof at the end of its life with no plan, a water heater from another decade, a crawlspace nobody has looked at, deferred maintenance stacked in the corners. Handle the fear items and the cosmetic stuff matters far less than people think. I will tell you which is which, and I will tell you which projects to skip.

Marketing that reaches the actual buyer

A Springfield starter home and forty acres outside Pleasant Hill do not have the same buyer, so they should not have the same marketing. In-town homes need great photography and syndication done right. Acreage needs drone coverage, mapped boundaries, documented water and septic, and copy that sells the life instead of listing the bedroom count.

The inspection period is where deals die

Or where they hold together. Sellers who did the diligence in advance are answering questions with paperwork instead of concessions. Sellers who did not are negotiating against a report they are reading for the first time. That difference routinely runs into five figures, and it is decided months before an offer ever comes in.

FAQ

Selling: straight answers

What is my home actually worth?

Not what an online estimate says. Those models cannot see your finishes, your shop, your view, your septic, or the fact that the comp down the street had a failing foundation. I build valuations from closed local sales plus the things that actually move price here. The walk-through and the analysis are free.

How long before listing should I start preparing?

Sixty to ninety days is the sweet spot for most homes. Long enough to knock out repairs without paying rush prices, short enough that you stay motivated. For rural property with well, septic, and outbuilding documentation to gather, give it longer.

Should I renovate the kitchen before selling?

Usually no. Full kitchen remodels rarely return their cost at resale, and you get zero enjoyment out of one you are leaving. Paint, lighting, hardware, deep cleaning, and landscaping return far better per dollar. I will give you the prioritized list.

Do I need to be out of the house to sell it?

No. Plenty of homes sell fully lived in. It takes more coordination on showings and more discipline on tidiness, but it is completely normal. If the house is vacant, we talk about whether staging pencils out.
