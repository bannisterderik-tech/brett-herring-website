Thurston: the east end, where springfield still feels like a town

Thurston was a town before it was a neighborhood, and it still carries itself that way. There was a post office here in 1877, a general store and a blacksmith at the corner of Thurston Road and 66th, and a farming community that had been going for decades before Springfield ever annexed it. Drive that intersection today and the bones are still visible. The Grange hall is still standing, still being used, and the hills behind it are still the reason people want to live on this side of town.

The property picture

What you will actually find.

The housing here leans detached single family, with the largest share built in the 1970s and a strong second wave through the 1990s and 2000s. That mix is what makes the east end work for a lot of buyers: you can find a mid-century ranch that needs your attention, a nineties subdivision home that does not, or an older place on a bigger lot toward the county line. Head far enough east on Thurston Road or up toward the hills and lots get larger, some with the well and septic that come with leaving city services behind.

Living here

Thurston Hills opened to the public in 2017 with more than twelve miles of trail, including the region's first purpose-built downhill mountain bike trails, cut in partnership with the local Disciples of Dirt crew. Lively Park sits on Thurston Road with thirty-one acres, a four-acre dog park, and an eight-thousand-square-foot playground. Splash!, the indoor wave pool attached to it, is the reason February is survivable with kids. And the McKenzie is right there.

Schools

Springfield Public Schools serves Thurston, including Thurston Elementary, Ridgeview Elementary, Thurston Middle School, and Thurston High School, home of the Colts since 1960. Walterville Elementary sits further east along the McKenzie Highway and calls itself a rural school, which it earns.

Local knowledge

Things you learn by living here.

  • The Grange hall on Thurston Road went up in 1912, built by roughly forty neighbors with lumber logged locally, and it was one of the first buildings in Oregon with an arched ceiling. It became Thurston Grange #853 in 1936 and it still hosts community functions.
  • Hendricks Bridge Park was a cable ferry crossing from 1862 to 1897 before it was ever a boat ramp. It is now the take-out for a lot of McKenzie float trips.
  • The McKenzie River drift boat, the design that changed river boats across the West, was developed on this river in the 1940s.
  • Willamalane is a separate parks district with its own tax base, created by voters in 1944. It is not the city. That distinction shows up on your tax statement.
